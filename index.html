00 / Statement Artist Statement

A collection of nine typographic narratives — bodies rendered in characters, stories told in code.

I am an Iranian-born artist working at the intersection of generative code and photographic portraiture. My work begins with a simple observation: the body, like a text, can be read — line by line, symbol by symbol. To see is to parse. To parse is to tell a story.

ASCII Body is a collection of nine works, each a feminine body photograph transformed into ASCII art using p5.js. The original images — photographed on a Nokia G20 — are mapped to a character set, so that light and shadow become slashes, dots, brackets, and letters. Some works read as portraits; others as landscapes, dreams, or love letters.

Each work carries a written story — a short prose piece that accompanies the composition. The stories range from mythic ("a generative homage to the divine feminine") to sensual ("a burst of forbidden desire") to tender ("a love letter in pixels, just for you"). Together they form a set of small fables told in characters and code.

On AI tools — where I encountered technical obstacles, I turned to AI tools for debugging and code optimization. Every structural, aesthetic, and conceptual decision remained my own.

03 / Process

Structure
behind the body.

01 / Characters

Typographic Vocabulary

Every work is drawn from a small set of characters: slashes, dots, brackets, letters, and punctuation. There is no raster image, no imported texture. Each symbol is placed by code, so the work is legible at any scale — from a print to a wall.

02 / Story

Each Work Has One

Every work carries a written story — a short prose piece that accompanies the composition. The stories range from mythic to sensual to tender. The collection is not just a set of images; it is a set of fables told in characters.

03 / The Body

Feminine Form

The collection is held together by the body — the feminine form, the act of looking, the transformation of flesh into syntax. The title comes from the sense that these are not drawings of objects, but of what the eye notices as it moves across a body.
